Best Rewards Credit Cards in 2026

May 2026 8 min read

Key Takeaways

  • The best rewards card depends on your biggest spending categories
  • Travel cards offer the highest potential value — but only if you'll use the rewards
  • Flat-rate cashback cards are simpler and often just as valuable for everyday spenders
  • Rewards cards only make financial sense if you pay your balance in full each month
  • Sign-up bonuses can be worth $200–$1,000 — but only count on them if you'd hit the spend naturally

The rewards credit card market in 2026 is more competitive than ever — great news for consumers. Whether you want straightforward cashback, airline miles, or a flexible points currency you can use anywhere, there's a card built for your spending habits.

This guide presents our top picks across every rewards category, with honest assessments of who each card suits best — and who should look elsewhere.

Our Top Rewards Cards for 2026

1

Chase Sapphire Preferred® Card — Best Overall

The Sapphire Preferred has held its place at the top of rewards card rankings for years. It earns 3x points on dining, 2x on travel, and 1x on everything else. The real power is in the transfer partners — Chase Ultimate Rewards points transfer 1:1 to United, Southwest, Hyatt, Marriott, and over a dozen other programmes, regularly delivering over 2¢ per point in value. A $95 annual fee is easy to justify for frequent travellers and diners.

3x Dining 2x Travel Transfer Partners $95 Annual Fee
2

American Express® Gold Card — Best for Dining & Groceries

If dining and groceries dominate your spending, the Amex Gold is hard to beat. It earns 4x Membership Rewards points at US restaurants and 4x at US supermarkets (up to $25,000 per year), plus 3x on flights booked directly. The $250 annual fee is offset by up to $120 in dining credits and $120 in Uber Cash annually, making the effective cost much lower for cardholders who use those benefits.

4x Dining & Groceries Amex Transfer Partners $250 Annual Fee
3

Capital One Venture Rewards Credit Card — Best Simple Travel Card

The Venture earns a flat 2x miles on every purchase — no categories to track, no quarterly activations. Miles are redeemable against any travel purchase at 1¢ each, or transferred to over 15 airline and hotel partners. For travellers who want solid rewards without complexity, the Venture is the clear choice. $95 annual fee.

2x Miles Everything 15+ Transfer Partners $95 Annual Fee
4

Wells Fargo Active Cash® Card — Best No-Fee Rewards Card

The simplest high-value rewards card available — unlimited 2% cashback on all purchases, no annual fee, no categories, no expiry. Also comes with a 0% intro APR period on purchases and balance transfers. The go-to choice for anyone who wants strong rewards without paying an annual fee or tracking spending categories.

2% Flat Cashback No Annual Fee 0% Intro APR
5

Chase Freedom Flex℠ — Best for Rotating Category Earners

Earns 5% on rotating quarterly categories (up to $1,500 per quarter, activation required), 3% on dining and drugstores, and 1% everywhere else. No annual fee. For cardholders willing to activate quarterly categories and shift spending accordingly, the earning potential is exceptional — especially when paired with a Chase Sapphire card to convert cashback into transferable points.

5% Rotating Categories 3% Dining No Annual Fee

How to Choose the Right Rewards Card

The best rewards card earns the most on what you actually spend — not what looks impressive on a comparison table. Before applying, spend five minutes categorising a recent month of spending. Your top two or three categories should drive your decision.

Heavy on dining and travel?

The Chase Sapphire Preferred or Amex Gold will almost certainly earn you more than a flat-rate card. The bonus categories multiply your earnings where you spend most.

Spread spending or want simplicity?

A flat 2% cashback card will match or beat a category card for most everyday spenders. No activation, no tracking, no complexity.

Not sure yet?

Start with a no-annual-fee flat-rate card. You can always upgrade or add a category card once you have a clearer picture of your spending patterns.

⚠️ The Golden Rule of Rewards Cards

Rewards cards are only profitable if you pay your full balance every month. A 22% APR interest charge on a carried balance will wipe out months of rewards earnings in a single statement. If there's any chance you'll carry a balance, prioritise a low-APR card over a rewards card.

Making the Most of Your Sign-Up Bonus

Most premium rewards cards offer a sign-up bonus — typically worth $200–$1,000 in rewards value — for meeting a minimum spend in the first 3 months. These can be genuinely lucrative, but a few rules apply:

  • Only count on a bonus you'd earn through normal spending — groceries, bills, subscriptions all count
  • Never overspend or take on debt to hit a bonus threshold
  • Plan timing around a period of naturally higher spending — a holiday, home project, or large planned purchase
  • Check whether the bonus is available if you've held the same card before — most issuers have rules about repeat bonuses
✅ Pro Tip — The Two-Card Strategy

Many experienced rewards earners use two cards: a category card for their highest-spend areas and a flat 2% card for everything else. This maximises earnings without the complexity of juggling multiple bonus categories across several cards.
