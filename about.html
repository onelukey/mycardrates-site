A note from the founder

Why I built a US credit card site from the UK

My name is Mike Lucas. I'm based in the United Kingdom — which makes me an unlikely person to be running a credit card resource for American consumers. But that distance, I've come to realise, is actually one of this site's biggest strengths.

I've had a long-standing interest in personal finance — both in the UK and across global markets. A few years ago, I started looking closely at the US credit card landscape. What struck me immediately was how sophisticated the rewards ecosystem is — cash back, travel points, sign-up bonuses, 0% balance transfer windows — and yet how poorly a lot of that complexity is explained to ordinary consumers.

Too many sites are written to convert you, not to inform you. Card comparison pages are designed around affiliate clicks. "Best of" lists are shaped by whoever pays the highest commission. The person sitting at the kitchen table trying to work out whether a balance transfer card will actually save them money is, too often, an afterthought.

MyCardRates.com is my answer to that. A free, independent, plain-English resource covering the full credit card spectrum — from premium rewards cards for frequent travellers to secured cards for people rebuilding their credit from scratch. No sign-up walls. No sales calls. No agenda beyond helping you make a better-informed decision about a financial product that, used well, can genuinely improve your financial life.

I'm not a financial advisor, and this site isn't financial advice. What it is, is honest information — written clearly, kept current, and built around the questions real people actually ask.

Mike Lucas
Founder, MyCardRates.com

Why an outside perspective matters

Being based in the UK means I have no card issuer relationships, no affiliate pressure shaping what I write, and no financial stake in which card you choose. I research and write about US credit cards purely as an informed outsider who believes American consumers deserve clearer, more honest financial education than the industry typically provides. Every guide on this site is written to inform you — not to push you toward a particular product.

Editorial standards

What this site stands for

Three principles guide every page on MyCardRates.com.

Full transparency

This site earns revenue through advertising. That is always clearly disclosed and never influences what we write, which cards we cover, or how we explain the risks alongside the benefits.

Education first

Every page is written to inform, not to sell. That means explaining when a card might not be right for you just as clearly as when it might be — because a bad financial decision serves nobody.

Kept current

Card terms, rates, and offers change constantly. Our guides are reviewed regularly to make sure the information you're reading reflects today's market — not last year's.

How we work

Where the information comes from

Every guide on this site is built from primary sources and direct research — not rewritten summaries of other blogs.

  • Card issuer terms and disclosures

    Rate ranges, fee structures, and reward terms are sourced directly from card issuers' own published Schumer Boxes and cardholder agreements — the same documents you'd sign.

  • Live market monitoring

    We track published APRs, sign-up bonus changes, and balance transfer offers across major US issuers regularly to keep our comparisons accurate and reflect what consumers are actually being offered.

  • Plain-English translation

    Credit card terms are often written for lawyers. We read the small print and translate it into language that any consumer — regardless of financial background — can understand and act on.

  • Honest about the downsides

    Credit cards carry real risks — high interest rates, fees, and the potential for debt. We cover that clearly on every relevant page, because an informed consumer is a protected consumer.
